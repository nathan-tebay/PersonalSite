Overview

The AutoRejection project was a side project that became a business concept. I worked on it for most of a year, building it with my business partner under the company MAP Equipment, in cooperation with Wild Goose Filling and several local breweries in the Bozeman area. It was the second machine I programmed with MAP Equipment, the first being a packaging machine capable of boxing 12 packs or applying PakTech carriers to 6 packs of beer with only one prototype. Both of these were controlled by Raspberry Pi single-board computers, with the UI presented over Wi-Fi.

Aside from the technical challenges, many business challenges made these projects full of hard-earned experience. Overall, working with Wild Goose Filling was an incredible experience that I had hoped would have turned into more; they ultimately bought 5 of the 10 total units sold. They had a separate version from the one pictured that integrated with the output feed of their existing canning lines, a design that required extensive prototyping to get working at 50+ cans per minute.

The AutoRejection system included a UI with historical graphs of beer weights, current can weights as they passed over the scale, and a calibration routine. Stored data can be downloaded to a connected device in CSV format for customer use. It also communicated with AWS IoT to allow for over-the-air updates and data storage in AWS RDS. It communicated with a load cell through a custom PCB I designed and programmed. The Raspberry Pi is encrypted using a ZYMKEY - ZYMBIT for security and real-time clock functionality. A UL-listed stainless steel cabinet houses separate 5V and 24V power supplies for the electronics and the machine's automation.

Challenges

  • Achieving stable, reliable weight measurements and can rejection at 50+ cans per minute required many iterations.
  • Building a simple, mobile-friendly dashboard for real-time monitoring and historical data over Wi-Fi.
  • AWS IoT integration for OTA updates and data storage, complicated by encryption requirements on the Raspberry Pi.
  • Designing for a harsh brewery environment, including moisture, temperature changes, and limited control over the Wild Goose canning line integration.
  • Developing a custom load cell PCB and firmware for accurate, cost-effective weight measurement required working with a local electrical engineer and PCB manufacturer.
  • Coordinating with Wild Goose Filling and brewery staff to ensure the system fit existing workflows.
  • Balancing technical goals with cost, manufacturability, and a physical design that was not ideal for rapid prototyping.
  • Adding security features like drive encryption too early made prototyping more difficult.

Learnings

  • It didn’t need to be this complex; it could have been implemented with an ESP32, a simpler UI, and no AWS integration.
  • Hardware projects depend on rapid prototyping and iteration to reach reliable performance.
  • Close collaboration with partners and customers is essential for successful integration.
  • Good products must balance technical performance with cost and manufacturability.
  • Security matters, but adding it too early can slow development.
  • Harsh environments require thoughtful enclosure, power, and material choices.
  • Bringing hardware products to market takes longer than expected due to testing, manufacturing, and partner alignment.
  • Supply chain issues, like Raspberry Pi shortages, can quickly become major project risks.
  • Document the process thoroughly; code alone does not capture the full value of the work.
  • In partnerships, protect your time and value—unpaid work without a contract creates strain.

Components

  • Node-RED flows for automation logic and AWS integration
  • StandAlone and WildGoose variants for weight measurement and rejection
  • Custom JavaScript for data processing and UI
  • UIBuilder dashboard for real-time monitoring
  • AWS IoT for data storage and OTA updates
  • Pneumatic rejection system for the canning line

Hardware

  • Arduino-based Load Cell PCB with custom firmware
  • Raspberry Pi with dual Wi-Fi network control
  • UL-listed stainless steel enclosure
  • Separate 5V and 24V power supplies
  • ZYMKEY (ZYMBIT) for encryption and RTC
